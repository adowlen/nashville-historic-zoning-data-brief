# Nashville's historic-zoning lane: most dated cases issue the same day, and the register's own Decision field changes meaning mid-series

**One-line summary:** Of the 9,171 historic-zoning permit cases Nashville accepted between December 7, 2011 and November 6, 2026, 8,337 (90.9 percent) carry an issued date and 59.8 percent of those issued the same day they were accepted; among the 3,352 cases that took at least a day, the median wait was 17 days, and cases accepted since 2021 are recorded as approved 90.1 percent of the time, against 1.4 percent for cases accepted in 2011-2020.

Start with the scale. The Metropolitan Historic Zoning Commission's permit register holds 9,171 cases accepted from December 7, 2011 to November 6, 2026 - about 611 cases a year (derived). Every case in the layer is the same case type, Historical Commission Permit; the work splits across 15 recorded subtypes. Machine Readable pulled the full layer from Metro's ArcGIS service on November 9, 2026; the pulled row count matches the server's own count for the layer exactly (9,171). One row is one permit case as recorded in the register. An issued date records that the case was issued in the system; this brief measures cases as recorded, not work performed on a building.

## Two clocks inside one lane

- Of the 9,171 cases, 8,337 (90.9 percent) carry both an accepted date and an issued date; 834 (9.1 percent) carry no issued date. No dated case has a negative acceptance-to-issuance gap, and no date in the layer is a sentinel or placeholder value (audit, verification.md).
- Counted over the 8,337 dated cases, the median gap is 0 days: 4,985 cases (59.8 percent) issued the same day they were accepted. The other 3,352 cases waited a median of 17 days; the 90th percentile of all dated cases is 32 days, 856 dated cases (10.3 percent) took more than 30 days, and 3.2 percent took more than 90 days. The longest recorded gap is 2,764 days.
- The subtype split carries the difference. Historic New Construction is the largest subtype (2,650 cases, 28.9 percent) and the slowest at scale: its 2,352 dated cases waited a median of 13 days and 16.5 percent took more than 30 days. The next two subtypes by volume - HC - Additions - General (2,191 cases) and HC - Out Building (1,612 cases) - each waited a median of 0 days on their dated bases (1,999 and 1,461 dated cases). Historic Alteration (1,061 cases, 999 dated) issued the same day in 90.2 percent of dated cases; Historic Demolition (865 cases, 803 dated) issued the same day in 87.7 percent.
- The two approval routes also run on different clocks. Cases recorded as Approved (1,181, all dated) waited a median of 20 days, and 365 of them (30.9 percent) took more than 30 days. Cases recorded as Administratively Approved (2,553, all dated) waited a median of 0 days and issued the same day in 80.4 percent of cases. This brief prints the recorded Decision values with their bases; the next section records why those values cannot be read the same way across the whole series.

## The approval question the register cannot answer for its full span

- As recorded over all 9,171 cases, Decision reads: Disapproved 5,416 (59.1 percent), Administratively Approved 2,553 (27.8 percent), Approved 1,181 (12.9 percent), and Deferred 21 (0.2 percent). Approved and Administratively Approved together are 3,734 cases (40.7 percent).
- That full-span share does not hold up inside the register itself. Of the 5,416 cases recorded as Disapproved, 4,602 carry an issued date - including the register's earliest case, accepted and issued on December 7, 2011. A second field, PermitStatus, is blank on 5,099 cases (55.6 percent), reads Issued on 3,796 (41.4 percent), Received on 255, and Deferred on 21; 62 cases read Issued in PermitStatus and Disapproved in Decision at the same time.
- The break is dated. Among cases accepted in 2011-2020 (5,106 cases), 73 (1.4 percent) are recorded as Approved or Administratively Approved. Among cases accepted in 2021-2026 (4,065 cases), 3,661 (90.1 percent) are. Year by year, the recorded approved share is 0.0 percent in every year from 2011 through 2019, 9.3 percent in 2020, and between 87.1 and 91.5 percent in each year from 2021 through 2026 (year panel, data/year_panel.csv). The brief therefore reports the approval share only on the post-2020 base, and reports the full-span Decision counter as a record of the field as recorded, not as a denial rate for 2011-2020.
- Human scale (derived): at the post-2020 recorded rate, about 9 in 10 cases accepted since 2021 are recorded as approved; at the register's overall pace of about 611 cases a year (derived), the dated share that waits more than 30 days (10.3 percent of dated cases) is roughly 57 cases a year (derived).

## Where the cases concentrate

- ZIP code 37206, parsed from the tail of the ADDRESS field, holds 3,472 cases (37.9 percent of all 9,171). The top three ZIP codes - 37206, 37212 (1,778), and 37205 (887) - together hold 6,137 cases (66.9 percent). 112 cases carry no ZIP in the ADDRESS field, and 109 cases carry no ADDRESS or APN at all.
- The register's 9,171 cases sit on 4,352 distinct parcel numbers (APN) and 5,034 distinct addresses, so repeat cases at the same property are a normal feature of the lane. The most frequent single address carries 33 cases (401 Oak St, Nashville, TN 37210) and the most frequent parcel carries 34; both counts are printed with their bases here and are not compared against citywide property counts, which this layer does not contain.

## Methods

Population: all 9,171 records in the Metropolitan_Historic_Zoning_Commission_Permits_view layer (ArcGIS FeatureServer layer 0, services2.arcgis.com/HdTo6HJqh92wn4D8), pulled November 9, 2026 with 1,000-record pagination; the pulled count was verified against the server's returnCountOnly count (9,171) at pull time. Dates are epoch-millisecond values in the service, converted to America/Chicago local dates; gaps are issued date minus accepted date in whole days, computed only for cases with both dates and a non-negative gap (8,337 cases; 0 negative gaps found). Decision and PermitStatus shares use the full 9,171-case denominator unless a different base is printed in the sentence. ZIP codes are parsed from the tail of the ADDRESS field (112 cases carry no five-digit ZIP at the ADDRESS tail; a further 11 addresses end in 00000 and are carried in the ZIP panel as recorded, not in any named ZIP share). No join to any other dataset was performed; APN and ADDRESS are reported as recorded. Category stability: CASE_TYPE_DESC holds one value across the layer; SUB_TYPE_DESC holds 15 values; the Decision field's recorded distribution changes sharply between the 2011-2020 and 2021-2026 acceptance cohorts (Section above), and the brief treats that as a change in the recorded field, not as a change in outcomes. Exposure: the layer contains only cases that entered the historic-zoning process; it contains no count of building work in historic districts that never entered the process, so no rate of historic-district work is computed. Full audit, counters, and verification: verification.md.

## What this might mean

One reading is that the lane runs two processes under one register: a same-day administrative route for alterations, demolitions, and similar subtypes, and a slower commission route whose clearest marker in the data is the Approved decision (median 20 days, 30.9 percent over 30 days) and the New Construction subtype (median 13 days). The strongest alternative explanation is that the dates record administrative entry rather than review time - that accepted and issued dates were often entered together when a case was filed or closed out - which would make the same-day share a recording pattern rather than a service speed; the register alone cannot separate the two, and the Decision field's mid-series change shows that at least one field in this layer has been recorded under more than one practice. Either way, the register's post-2020 Decision values and its full-span timing values are the parts a reader can use as recorded; its pre-2021 Decision values cannot support a denial-rate claim.
